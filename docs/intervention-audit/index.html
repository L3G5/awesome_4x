Stage 6 · ASTERION · postgame audit

A reachable raid, a defended victory

Veyr could legally reach Concord's lead before the finish, using its existing movement allowance. The AI rejected the expedition because it plans and executes only one jump per cycle. The available raider nevertheless loses to the actual prepared garrisons.

Narrow recommendation: make an assigned intervention expedition use its real movement allowance, with a matching travel estimate, and traverse intermediate enemy colonies without automatically stopping to conquer them. Keep declaration thresholds, treaty rules, observed-strength checks and the home-threat policy unchanged. This evidence supports competent execution; it does not support guaranteeing an interruption or raising general aggression.

Evidence recovered without inventing earlier states

The original human-style playtest saved its cycle 40 starting checkpoint and exported cycle 67 ending, plus DOM observations, decisions and screenshots. Intermediate states were recovered by replaying 43 normal player commands through the archived Stage 6 engine. The replayed ending equals the actual UI export across every field, including RNG, all factions and fleets, exploration, logs, public dispatches and resources. All 27 recorded completed-cycle resource bars also match.

Only three selected intermediate states are retained here: before rival actions at cycles 60, 63 and 64. The portable script regenerates the entire audited sequence in memory, then examines each rival at its actual place in the Veyr → Khar → Choir turn order. The distinct automated-policy cycle 50/55 saves were never used.

The factual cycle 60 opportunity

Concord has reached 3/10 alignment, leaving seven advancing cycles before victory. Veyr's own network is still at 0/10. Vanguard 59 is at Veyr Prime with three frigates, 102 hull, 45 effective power and two moves. Veyr has 18 orders, 7,003 credits and 213 minerals, with positive income, no observed threat to its colonies, no Concord truce, and an enabled declaration action.

All eight jumps to Solace are charted and traversable after an ordinary declaration. Veyr has no sensors at any Concord beacon: the destination's guard is unknown, not known to be absent. The policy's existing 30-power allowance permits planning into that uncertainty. It does not inspect the hidden garrison. The actual rejection is 8 hops > 7 remaining cycles, even though Distributed Command permits two moves per cycle.

CYCLE 61Sable → Hesper → Ashfall

Tethys and Haven are intermediate Concord colonies. Movement rules allow passage through them. The current generic military controller instead attacks or invades whenever a fleet begins a later turn over hostile sovereignty, which can consume the expedition's remaining time. Fixing the estimate alone would therefore create promises the executor still cannot keep.

Normal-command counterfactuals

These are explicitly branches from the verified cycle 60 state, not events from the played campaign. The branch declares war through the normal diplomacy command, then spends at most two legitimate movement commands per cycle on Vanguard 59. Other AI turns continue normally. Concord retains its recorded construction and reinforcement orders. No fleet is teleported, no moves are reset, no resources are granted and no hidden defender is used to choose a route.

Why the other rivals did not stop Concord

FactorVerified finding
Before the visible leadCycles 48–55 have no completed Concord beacon; 56–57 reveal one and then two. Alignment begins at 58. The existing intervention threshold is progress ≥3, first reached at 60. This was not 17 cycles of ignored imminent victory.
TreatiesTrade and high trust are not immunity. Every rival's declaration on Concord is legally enabled throughout the audited period. Race intervention may break trade immediately, paying its normal diplomatic consequences. No special treaty exemption explains the peace.

It has no home threat at 61–64, so the global threat veto is not the continuing explanation. Its later declaration on Veyr also follows a reachable public race target. The intervention system is acting on feasible foreign races, rather than universally ignoring them.
ChoirIts four-frigate force is gone by the time Choir acts at 58; it has no military fleet at 58–60. Its only shipyard was occupied by Lumen's beacon until 58. It then rebuilds one frigate by 61 and a second by 64. At the declaration check on 64, the two separate fleets each have only 15 power, below the existing 30 threshold; merging happens later in the military phase. Even a merged two-frigate expedition would face five charted jumps to Elysian and six defending Concord frigates. A shortage of functioning military capacity, not lack of money, is decisive.
Own racesChoir, Veyr and Khar begin alignment at 62, 63 and 64 respectively. All trail Concord. Relative own progress does not disqualify a Concord intervention. Their expected uninterrupted finish dates would be 71, 72 and 73, so a credible raid could matter strategically.
Intelligence and strengthNone of the rivals has sensors at Concord's beacon sites during the evaluated opportunity. They can know locations, routes and public progress, but cannot know the garrisons remotely. Postgame knowledge that the defense wins must not become an AI eligibility check.

Scope and acceptance criteria for the next change

Assign the selected expedition to a specific fleet and public beacon target. Calculate arrival from current movement, subsequent normal movement and available orders; execute ordinary hops while retaining that assignment. Pass through intermediate hostile colonies when the route remains viable. Reassess on newly observed opposition, combat, changed access or an invalid target. Other fleets retain their ordinary defensive responsibilities.

The cycle 60 Veyr snapshot is a useful regression: the eight-hop Solace expedition is temporally feasible under Command and must not be rejected solely as eight cycles. A successful block is not the acceptance criterion. The unchanged defenders should still be allowed to win. Preserve one-move behavior, unknown-world restrictions, normal declaration cost/order spending, genuine home-defense priorities, and the rule that hidden fleet changes cannot affect pre-contact targeting.

The risks are overcommitting a small navy, rushing into unknown defenders, or executing too many irreversible hops after combat. A bounded expedition with explicit reassessment addresses those risks without granting intelligence, free movement or guaranteed success. Broader changes to grievance thresholds, treaty betrayal, global threat allocation, fleet merging and economic spending are not justified by this one run.
