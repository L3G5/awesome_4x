01
Your first expedition

Begin with an achievable ambition: discover a suitable neighbor, settle a second world, and give that world a useful role.

Take stock at Solace.

You begin with 140 credits, 110 minerals, and 6 Orders. Solace has 4 population, an Orbital Extractor, and an Orbital Shipyard. Your starting fleet includes a Pathfinder, a Colony Ark, and a Vanguard.

Choose your first research.

Deep-space Logistics is a practical opening: completing it adds one order every turn and opens the engineering branch. Adaptive Biospheres is useful when the nearby worlds are harsh.

Send a Pathfinder along a hyperlane.

Select the scout, click an adjacent star to preview the destination, then use Move. A Pathfinder can make two jumps in a turn, provided you also have enough Orders.

Read the arrival survey.

Arriving automatically surveys a system and charts its neighboring stars. Inspect habitability, mineral richness, and science potential. Your starting technology permits settlement at 50% habitability or better.

Prepare a second economy.

Move the Colony Ark to a suitable unclaimed world, then settle for 30 credits, 20 minerals, and an order. The Ark is consumed. An Orbital Extractor supports further construction; an Exchange Port funds a growing fleet; a Research Enclave accelerates technologies.

End the turn, then reassess.

Check what completed, what your neighbors did, and whether your income still supports your plans. New resources and refreshed Orders let you continue the expedition.

Try this first turn. Choose Logistics for free. Send the Pathfinder to a habitable adjacent world, move the Ark there, and settle: three Orders. Commission a Research Enclave at Solace and an Extractor at the new colony: two more. You retain one order, 60 credits, and 35 minerals before the next income. Use that final order for a useful scout jump or an available anomaly.

02
Orders & the turn

Your economy measures what you can afford. Orders measure how much of it you can direct at once.

The Concord starts with 6 Orders: a base of 4, one for its colony, and one from its Civic mandate. Each additional colony increases your next turn’s allowance by one, up to a total cap of 18. Unused Orders do not carry over.

Construction and charter cards show the change to your usable empire-wide Orders under today’s conditions. A Civic upgrade at the cap can still add credits while adding zero usable Orders. Capacity above 18 acts as a buffer: replacing a Forum or switching away from a Civic charter may leave your mandate unchanged. The forecast includes any charter lost when its supporting building is replaced.

Moving one jump, settling, starting construction, resolving an anomaly, taking a diplomatic action, attacking, merging or splitting fleets, assigning or clearing a defensive lead, or ordering emergency repairs each costs one order. Selecting research, cancelling construction, plotting or clearing a course, and Hold/Resume are free. Continuing a course pays for its actual jumps.

A ship’s remaining moves are a separate limit. Moving and attacking each use one move as well as an order; settling does not require a remaining move. Having spare Orders does not give an exhausted fleet another jump or attack.

IMMEDIATE DECISIONS
Give a command

Select the relevant fleet or world, inspect the action and its cost, then commit. Fleet destinations are previewed before you press Move.

LONG-TERM CAPACITY
Grow your mandate

Deep-space Logistics adds one order per turn. Distributed Command adds two. Each completed Civic Forum contributes another order.

What happens when you end a turn

Rival civilizations act, then income and research are applied. Colonies grow and advance construction; eligible fleets repair. Fleet moves and each empire’s Orders refresh for the next turn. An ongoing construction project does not need another order each turn.

The Cycle briefing in the top bar records the latest turn’s threats, diplomatic developments, completed projects, and discoveries. It stays available after closing it or reloading your campaign. Use its links to inspect a world, choose research, or open diplomacy. Battles during the rivals’ turn open the briefing automatically.

Spend your strongest turns on actions that unlock future options: another colony, a shipyard in the right place, or exploration that establishes a useful route. A growing empire benefits from more Orders as well as more income.

Before ending a turn: check idle fleets, your research selection, colony construction, and the credits needed to maintain your fleet.

03
The unknown galaxy

Hyperlanes connect the stars. Exploration turns a possible destination into a place you can understand, use, and defend.

Move fleets one jump at a time between adjacent, charted systems. Arrival automatically surveys the destination and reveals its neighboring stars. A charted system is visible on your map; a surveyed system has the detailed information gained by visiting it.

Scouts are fast but fragile: the Pathfinder has two moves, 18 hull, and only 3 base attack. Pathfinders and Colony Arks cannot enter systems occupied by hostile fleets. Peaceful foreign borders require a trade treaty for passage.

Plot a course, then give the order

Select a fleet and a distant charted star to preview a route. Plot course is free and remembers the path between cycles. The map draws a dashed preview and a solid plotted course, with numbered stops. The fit-course control frames the route above the command panel.

Continue explicitly spends up to the jumps and Orders shown, one of each per jump. It never moves the fleet automatically when you end a turn. Routine surveys can continue within that allowance, but the fleet stops for first contact, a newly discovered anomaly or restored site, combat, or interception.

A visible hostile fleet stops the course before entry; select that system and deliberately engage if you want to fight. Hidden opposition remains unknown until encountered. Closed borders retain the plotted course; use Replot to find a currently accessible path. Clearing or replanning a course is free, including with no Orders left. A direct move along the next leg advances the plan; a different move clears it.

Charts are not live sensors

Charted sovereignty remains public, and your surveys preserve a world's environmental details. Foreign fleets and exact combat strength are visible only in systems containing one of your colonies or fleets. An Unscouted combat estimate means opposition is unknown, not absent. Rival admirals follow the same sensor rules.

A visible blockade prevents civilian movement without spending an order. If a Pathfinder or Colony Ark attempts a jump into a hidden hostile fleet, it uses one move and one order, detects the interception, and withdraws to its starting system. It does not survey the destination or leave a permanent sensor there.

A world is more than its color

Inspect a system’s habitability, ownership, and survey status before sending an Ark. Worlds range from temperate Terran planets and ocean worlds to glacial, volcanic, arid, and barren environments. Your technology determines which environments you can settle.

Discoveries have a choice

Each stage of a discovery offers a permanent choice. Investigating costs one order and requires either your colony or a fleet at the surveyed system. You cannot investigate another empire’s anomaly, and hostile warships must be cleared first. Read both options before committing: one might solve an immediate shortage, while another changes the long-term value of a world.

THE HOLLOW SUN
Materials or an escort

Redirect ancient foundries for 85 minerals, or complete an ancient frigate that joins your expedition.

GARDEN BETWEEN STARS
A world or a windfall

Wake the caretakers for up to 25 additional habitability points, capped at 100%, and 20 science; or harvest 65 credits and 25 minerals. The choice shows the actual habitability increase.

A Thousand Sleeping Voices offers refugee arrival at your current capital for 20 credits. The choice names the receiving world and shows its resulting population. Refugees can exceed the natural-growth limit: an 8/8 world becomes 9/8, retains those citizens and their ordinary contributions, and pauses natural growth until capacity exceeds population. If your capital is no longer an owned colony, this choice adds no population; restoring the refugees’ navigation remains the alternative.

The Echo of the First Gate offers a different trade: 75 science, or a chart of every star within two jumps of the discovery. Already charted stars add no new chart information. Information can help you choose where your next fleet should go.

Two patient worlds

Every new galaxy contains one Last Observatory and one Patient Reef, among its existing discovery worlds. Both offer an immediate reward or a longer restoration that remains with the world through conquest.

The record you carry

Expedition records preserve the choice, cycle, civilization and actual outcome, including capped habitability gains and recovered ships. The Hollow Sun’s escort is named Last Light of its discovery world. Its Fleet heritage follows mergers as a historical record, even when the original hull may no longer survive.

Distant discovery records retain your last observation. An old survey does not reveal a rival’s later choices, maturation or restoration. Returning to the world updates the record. Older saves keep resolved discoveries resolved; if their detailed choice was never recorded, the game says so without inventing an outcome.

04
Worlds & economy

A colony becomes valuable through what it produces and where it lets your fleets operate.

A Colony Ark establishes a colony on an unclaimed world at its current location. Settling costs 30 credits, 20 minerals, and one order; the Ark is consumed and the colony begins with one population. Clear any hostile fleet first. The initial habitability threshold is 50%; Adaptive Biospheres reduces it to 30%. Worldshaping permits colonization of any world and increases colony growth by 50%.

Credits & minerals

Construction requires both resources up front. Warships and civilian vessels also have credit upkeep every turn. Balance a new purchase against the income needed to maintain it.

Production & growth

A colony produces 4 construction work plus its population each turn. A Manufactory adds 6 more. Habitability affects growth and population capacity. Habitation Domes add 4 capacity and improve growth by 40%.

Each colony builds one project at a time, and each improvement can be built once per colony. Costs are paid when you commission the project. Cancelling refunds the unused fraction of its credits and minerals; production already spent is not refunded.

The Empire table compares each owned world’s credits, minerals, science and production per cycle, alongside its specialty and construction. Select a column heading to sort; select it again to reverse the order. These are local outputs before fleet upkeep and trade. Use Inspect to plan a project at a promising world.

Make room for a purpose

Every building, including a Beacon, occupies one infrastructure slot. Colonies have two slots, increasing to three at population 3, four at population 6, and five at population 9. Ships and charters do not occupy additional slots.

When slots are full, choose a building to replace in the construction dialog. The old building keeps working until its replacement finishes. Cancelling the project keeps the old building. Population loss never demolishes infrastructure, and existing campaigns retain buildings already above the new limit.

Give a world a charter

A colony may have one charter, with two levels. Establishing level I requires population 4, Deep-space Logistics, and the supporting building; it costs 120 credits, 60 minerals, 60 work, and one order. Upgrading to level II requires population 8 and Autonomous Industry, costing 300 credits, 100 minerals, 100 work, and one order.

Benefits per charter level; the construction dialog shows this world’s exact values.
CHARTER / SUPPORT	BENEFITS PER LEVEL
Extraction / Orbital Extractor	Mineral income increases by 2 plus the world’s ore richness.
Academy / Research Enclave	Science income increases by 2 plus the world’s native science.
Forge / Manufactory	Production increases by 4 plus one per four population, rounded down. Military ship mineral costs fall by 10% per level, with the final price rounded up. The colony’s shared repair pool gains 2 hull per cycle.
Civic / Civic Forum	One additional empire order, within the 18-order limit, and 2 credits plus one per two population, rounded down.

Switching specialties commissions a new level-I charter. Your existing charter works until the new one finishes, then its investment is replaced without a refund. Replacing its supporting building removes the charter when construction completes. Captured charters remain in place. Charters have no recurring upkeep.

Compare an investment

Open Compare investment beneath a building or charter commission to compare the current colony with its completed project. The panel shows local income, production, population capacity, infrastructure use and usable empire Orders, including losses from a replacement and any dependent charter. Changes to defenses, repair capacity, military mineral discounts and owned Beacons appear when relevant. The ordinary commission card still starts the project directly.

The project’s own construction estimate uses current production. A new Manufactory or Forge cannot accelerate its own construction. The separate ship and Beacon table shows the time and price for one later commission at each production rate; unused production does not carry into another project. Removing a Shipyard makes ship construction unavailable. Money, Orders or a busy queue can prevent commissioning while still allowing comparison.

All comparisons hold population and technology fixed. Growth, research and later events can change completion time and income. Existing benefits remain through construction, and new recurring income is collected from the cycle after completion. Removing Habitation Domes retains existing population even above the lower capacity; natural growth pauses until capacity exceeds population. Repair is a shared capacity, subject to hostile blockade. More Beacons still require an eligible, funded alignment network.

Build for the next constraint

An Extractor is useful when minerals limit your expansion. An Exchange Port supports credit income. A Research Enclave produces science. An Orbital Shipyard enables local ship construction and raises the colony’s base repair capacity from 4 to 8 hull per cycle.

Colony improvement reference

Watch your net income. The income figures already include fleet upkeep. If your treasury reaches zero while credit income is negative, a ship is decommissioned. Exchange Ports and trade income can help restore a sustainable budget.

05
Research & progress

Choose a technology for the change it makes possible. The research tree links economic capacity, fleet strength, and the ancient network.

Choose one active research project. Science income and discovery rewards advance it; science left over is banked for later. Choosing or changing a project costs no Orders. Changing projects returns its invested progress to your science stock, so you do not lose that investment.

AN EXPANDING CIVILIZATION
Logistics & biospheres

Deep-space Logistics increases Orders. Adaptive Biospheres opens harsher worlds. Their branches lead to factories, better growth, and Civic Forums.

A CONTESTED FRONTIER
Lances & deflectors

Coherent Lances increases warship damage by 25%. Deflector Fields reduces incoming fleet damage by 20%. Capital Ship Architecture unlocks cruisers.

For the ancient network, follow Resonance Theory → Quantum Archives → the Asterion Protocol. The Protocol also requires Autonomous Industry, which follows Deep-space Logistics. That scientific route depends on an economy able to construct the Beacons it unlocks.

06
Fleets & warfare

A fleet is an investment you must build, supply with credits, and keep alive.

Build ships at a colony with an Orbital Shipyard. Pathfinders explore; Colony Arks settle. Vanguards provide economical combat strength, and Sovereign cruisers carry heavier weapons and much more hull.

With a military fleet selected at its current owned Shipyard, the inspector offers a direct commission for another ship of that class. It shows the current credits, minerals, one-order cost and build time, including Forge discounts. Completion creates a separate formation; you decide whether to merge it later. A full task force can still commission a new ship. Each commission is a separate decision and payment.

Prepare before committing

Declare war before fighting another empire. Moving into a system occupied by its fleets triggers combat automatically. Only the selected formation attacks; nearby friendly formations do not combine their fire. With current sensors, the system inspector lists opposing formations in the order this command will encounter them. Without sensors, their presence remains unknown.

Each engagement pairs one attacker with one defender for up to four rounds, with both dealing damage each round. A surviving attacker proceeds to the next hostile formation, even if the previous defender also survives. If the attacker is destroyed, the sequence stops. One attack command costs one move and one order for that sequence; another attack requires another move and order. Entering orbit or fighting fleets does not invade the colony.

Your formations defend separately in the order listed in orbit, including civilian ships. Later defenders fight only if the attacker survives the earlier exchanges. Each battle report records the named participants and their actual losses. The strength estimate is a rough comparison, not a casualty forecast or a guarantee.

Founding worlds and conquest

Domination requires all four founding worlds at once. Empire lists each founding world you own or have surveyed, its founding civilization when known, and its current controller. Select a world to inspect it. The map marks recognized objectives with ⌂ and a diamond ring; beacon and course markings remain separate.

Contact or a charted starlane alone does not identify a founding world. Unidentified objectives remain anonymous, and rival control totals say confirmed until all four worlds are identified. Your own control total is exact. These four objectives never move when a defeated government relocates its administrative capital to another colony.

Choose a defensive lead

Select one of your military formations in orbit, then use Lead defense in the system inspector. Assigning, replacing or clearing the lead costs one order, with no movement, credits or minerals. It works after that fleet has spent its movement. One formation per government can lead at each system; its badge and position in the orbital list show the assignment.

The lead takes your first defensive exchange. It provides no combat bonus and does not guarantee protection: a surviving attacker can still reach later defenders, even when an earlier defender survives. Choosing a heavy ship first can spare escorts but leave your expensive cruiser damaged or destroyed. Other governments retain their own places in the encounter sequence.

The assignment persists through cycles, saves, Hold/Resume, repairs and local combat while the formation survives. Leaving the system, including forced withdrawal, clears it. Splitting keeps the lead on the original formation; merging transfers it to the survivor if either input was the lead. Hold controls readiness and remains independent. Rival governments use the same paid command with remaining orders after their other work.

A visible hostile fleet changes the movement command to Engage, including at neutral stars. After a battle, an operation report shows each side’s ships and hull before and after the engagement, losses, and survivors. Reopen recent reports from the Chronicle; the campaign retains the latest 50 reports involving your empire.

Read the exchanges

New fleet reports include Inspect exchanges beneath the outcome cards. Open it to see each round’s outgoing volley, ships and hull before and after, and the weapons, faction and shield effects used in that battle. Both formations fire from their strength at the start of the round, so a ship destroyed during an exchange still fires. Ship losses reduce the following round’s firepower.

A volley is shown before the target’s shields. Shields reduce incoming fire by 20%; actual hull loss is limited by the hull remaining. The display rounds values, so small differences are normal. After four rounds, surviving formations remain in orbit; the attacking formation can still face the next defender. Reports retain their original names and modifiers after repairs, research or fleet destruction. Older reports retain their outcome without reconstructed exchanges, and planetary invasions keep their separate bombardment account.

Combine your same-type fleets in the same system for one order, up to 12 ships in a task force. Merging keeps the lower remaining movement allowance of the two fleets. Colony Arks cannot merge.

If exactly one compatible formation is present, Merge combines it immediately; the inspector names that partner and previews the result. If several are available, Merge fleets opens a chooser. The selected fleet keeps its identity, plotted course and Hold state. The absorbed fleet’s separate instructions end. A defensive lead transfers from either formation, and expedition records are retained without repeating their rewards. Merging preserves hull; it does not repair damage or increase the colony’s shared repair pool.

Choose Split fleet to detach ships for one order, leaving at least one ship in each formation. The preview shows both hull totals and their remaining jumps. Damage is divided proportionally; total ships, hull and upkeep stay the same. Both formations retain their existing movement allowance. The original keeps its name and plotted course, while the detached fleet starts without a course. Historical expedition records follow both branches and combine again without duplication if they reunite.

Separate formations can defend different worlds or take different routes. They also fight separately: dividing a force changes the sequence of engagements and may change casualties. Splitting does not promise the same battle outcome as keeping the fleet together.

Capture a world

Clear hostile fleets, then use the attack action while orbiting the enemy colony. Bombardment reduces its defenses and the colony returns fire. You capture it when defenses reach zero and your attacking fleet survives. Capturing reduces population by one, to a minimum of one, and cancels its construction project.

The Invade preview shows the colony’s remaining defense, your damage range, expected retaliation, remaining hull, and whether capture is possible or guaranteed if you attack now. It explicitly warns when retaliation will destroy the formation; clearing all defenses does not capture a colony if the attacker is destroyed. The resulting report records actual defense damage, retaliation, and ownership. Each bombardment costs one order and one remaining move.

Base colony defense is 12 plus 3 per population. Original capital worlds add 15; an Orbital Bastion adds another 30. A colony recovers 5 defense each turn when no hostile fleet is in orbit, so maintaining pressure matters.

Repair and regroup

Each owned colony supplies a shared pool of 4 hull repairs per cycle, or 8 with an Orbital Shipyard, plus any Forge charter bonus. If no hostile fleet is present, its damaged fleets share that capacity in proportion to their missing hull. The system inspector previews each share. Splitting or merging at the same colony does not increase total recovery; dispersing between colonies gives access to their separate repair capacity. Emergency repairs still restore up to 24 hull immediately for 10 minerals and one order. Repairs cannot replace ships already destroyed.

Coherent Lances improves damage by 25%, and Deflector Fields reduces incoming damage by 20%. Distributed Command gives warships a second move, allowing them to move and attack in the same turn when Orders permit.

A fleet needs an economy behind it. Four Vanguards cost 8 credits every turn in upkeep. Before expanding a task force, check that your income can support it alongside your next construction project.

Public affairs across the Reach

The public-affairs panel shows wars and treaties between other governments. Each civilization’s diplomatic card separately describes its relations with the Concord: two powers can both be peaceful toward you while fighting each other.

A contacted government broadcasts declarations of war, signed peace and trade agreements, and trade cancellation deadlines. If you have not contacted the other party, its identity remains anonymous. First contact reveals the current diplomatic situation; it does not invent announcements from before your envoys arrived. Gifts, unaccepted peace offers, private grievances and military plans remain private.

When a charted world changes sovereign, a dated dispatch identifies its new government. These reports do not survey the world or reveal its fleets, defenses or battle losses. Uncharted conquests do not reveal new locations. A public transfer can close a plotted course if its new ruler denies you access; inspect the affected world, negotiate a treaty or replot through accessible space.

Received announcements retain the names and facts you knew at the time, even after later contact. The current relationship overview can show newly identified governments.

Trade treaty: costs 30 credits and requires peace and trust of at least zero. Both sides gain open borders and 6 credits per turn; Stellar Accords raises your treaty income to 10. Signing adds 10 trust. A quiet trading relationship improves trust by one per cycle, but border grievances can outweigh that benefit.
Diplomatic gift: transfers 35 credits to the other empire and improves mutual trust by 20. Gifts are available during peace; they do not erase existing grievances.
End trade: gives three cycles of notice for one order. Trade income and border access continue during the notice period. At its end, both stop and visiting fleets withdraw.
Declare war: opens hostile territory to your fleets and reduces mutual trust by 40. Breaking an active trade treaty increases the trust penalty to 65 and gives the betrayed empire a larger grievance. A signed truce prevents either side from declaring war until it expires.
Propose peace: negotiations open after five cycles of war, but the other empire can refuse. War duration, lost colonies, and military pressure they can observe affect willingness. The Diplomacy view explains their current position. An accepted proposal pays 60 credits to the other empire.
A trade offer needs your agreement

A rival proposing trade reserves the 30-credit treaty fee. Accept or decline before the expiry cycle shown on the offer. Borders and income stay unchanged while the proposal is pending. Acceptance uses one order and no credits from your treasury. The reserved fee establishes the treaty; both empires gain border access and the usual trade benefits.

Declining uses one order and refunds the proposer; ignoring the offer lets it expire with the same refund. After refusal, expiry or completed treaty cancellation, that rival waits five cycles before sending another unsolicited trade proposal. You may still initiate a treaty yourself for the usual 30 credits and one order. Existing treaties remain active when you restore an older campaign.

A peace offer is your decision

An AI empire offering peace reserves 60 credits and sends terms for you to accept or decline. The war continues, and your fleets stay where they are, until you accept. Respond before the expiry cycle shown on the offer; refusal or expiry returns the reserved money to its sender. Responding uses one order.

An offer marked Due before cycle 12 must be answered during cycle 11 at the latest. Ending cycle 11 lets it expire.

A funded offer remains available until its deadline even if the battlefield changes. Diplomacy shows these binding terms while you decide. When no offer is pending, its willingness explanation describes a new proposal in which you would pay the other empire.

An accepted settlement gives the recipient the payment, resets mutual trust to −5, and creates a five-cycle mutual truce. Fleets inside each other's territory return to their nearest owned colony and lose remaining movement. If an empire has no colony, a charted neutral system can serve as its withdrawal point.

Trust and grievances

Trust measures diplomatic goodwill; grievances record conflicts of interest. New neighboring claims and observed military pressure create grievances. A quiet frontier lets them ease. The Khar Dominion also contests shared borders: its grievance rises by one each peaceful cycle on a shared frontier. Once its grievance reaches 24, a sufficiently prepared Khar force can give notice to end trade and prepare for war. The other powers require greater grievance before taking that course. Warnings and treaty deadlines appear in Diplomacy and the chronicle.

A peaceful frontier frees fleets and Orders for expansion elsewhere. An economic lead can also finance the ships you will need if negotiations stop serving your interests.

08
A lasting civilization

Turn a growing empire into an outcome. Make your long-term objective part of every major investment.

Restore the ancient network

Research the Asterion Protocol, hold three colonies with Asterion Beacons, and complete ten alignment cycles to achieve transcendence. Each Beacon costs 180 credits and 180 minerals, requires 140 work, and uses one infrastructure slot.

Alignment advances once per completed cycle and costs 60 credits after ordinary income arrives. A hostile military fleet over a participating beacon, fewer than three owned beacons, or insufficient funds interrupts it. Each interrupted cycle loses one alignment step, to a minimum of zero. Civilian ships alone cannot blockade the network. Captured beacons still require the Protocol. The network retains its three participating worlds; a spare fills a vacancy if ownership or a beacon is lost. A spare cannot bypass an existing participant’s blockade.

Funding at current income in Empire separates your treasury, ordinary income and the 60-credit alignment requirement. For example, 50 credits plus 75 income can fund alignment and leave 65 credits. The top-bar income is before alignment payments. This is a current-conditions estimate: rival actions, treaty changes, research and construction can change the result before alignment resolves.

The Empire view shows the public beacon signals and alignment progress of civilizations you have contacted. Their beacon locations become charted, but environmental surveys and foreign fleets still require an expedition or local sensors. The Empire navigation badge shows the leading rival’s progress. A new rival alignment or a rival reaching eight steps opens your briefing; from five steps, the galaxy view also highlights the race. Use these signals to decide whether to accelerate your own network, defend its worlds, or interrupt a rival.

Rival expeditions may collect compatible warships along their route to a Beacon. Their ships still spend ordinary orders and movement. Watch the force you can currently observe; an earlier sighting does not guarantee its strength when it arrives.

Plan the whole project. Three Beacons require 540 credits and 540 minerals, plus at least 600 credits for successful alignment. Budget for the colonies, infrastructure, and fleets that protect them.

If multiple civilizations finish alignment in the same cycle, the one with the higher Legacy score wins. An exact score tie uses a deterministic draw from the galaxy seed. The live public-network view repeats this rule and shows your own Legacy score; it does not disclose private rival scores.

Unite the original capitals

A domination victory requires ownership of all four original capitals: Solace, Veyr Prime, Kharos, and Lumen. These specific worlds remain the objectives even if an empire relocates its government.

Leave the greatest legacy

If no empire has won earlier, the surviving civilization with the highest score on turn 120 wins a legacy victory. Score adds 20 per colony, 2 per population, 12 per completed technology, 80 per Beacon, and one point per 4 military power, rounded down.

Rivals can win by the same routes. Losing every colony does not eliminate an empire while it still has a Colony Ark; losing both its colonies and its Arks ends its civilization.

Review the completed age

The conclusion records the winner, victory type and cycle, alongside your final worlds, population, ships, technologies and Legacy. Reopen it from the final map message, the game menu or the Chronicle, including after importing a finished campaign. Inspect the galaxy, follow retained battle and expedition records, or export the exact campaign save before beginning another age.

Selected retained moments come from the records still in the save. The Chronicle retains up to 160 entries and combat history up to 50 player-involved reports; the selection is not a complete timeline or a lifetime casualty total. Older saves may record a Transcendence victory without alignment metadata. Their recorded result remains valid, and missing progress is shown as unavailable.

09
Controls & saves

The right panel follows your command view. Galaxy inspects the selected system; Research shows your current project and contributing worlds; Empire lists upcoming construction; Chronicle opens learned expedition records. In Diplomacy, choose Civilization dossier to read about a contacted government, then View diplomatic actions to return to its treaty controls.

Fleet portraits distinguish scouts, colony arks, frigates and cruisers. Ancient trim marks inherited expedition history; it does not guarantee that every recovered hull still survives. Tactical map symbols remain compact for selecting fleets in orbit.

Drag the map to pan and use the mouse wheel to zoom. Select a fleet, then click an adjacent star to preview a move. Press Move to commit. Selecting a destination alone does not send the fleet.

Open the fleet roster from the fleet command bar to find every formation, including forces beyond the current view. Selecting one focuses its location. Next fleet and N cycle through fleets awaiting orders: they need an available jump, an available order, and no Hold position instruction. When that queue is empty, the roster still lets you inspect and select any fleet.

Hold position acknowledges a deliberate garrison or observation post. It is free, persists through cycles and saves, and removes the fleet from the ready queue and unfinished-fleet warning. Movement and combat rules stay the same. Choose Resume to put it back in the queue, or issue a manual move, attack, course continuation or new course. Inspection, repair and clearing a course preserve Hold. The original formation keeps its hold when splitting or merging; a new detachment starts active.

A held fleet returns to attention after its own combat, forced relocation, or a newly observed hostile fleet or hostile colony at its position. A distant war announcement does not wake every garrison, and a hostile already present when you choose Hold does not repeatedly wake it. The cycle warning still calls out genuinely unassigned research; a completed technology tree needs no acknowledgement.

Keep your civilization

The game saves automatically after each action. Use the menu’s export and import controls to keep a separate save or move your game between browsers. Export a copy before clearing browser data if you want to retain the campaign.

The game is turn based. Taking time to inspect a world, compare a research choice, or read the guide does not advance the simulation.
