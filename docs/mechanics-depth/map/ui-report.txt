What this does and does not demonstrate
The changes went beyond world names: first research capability, the role of the first colony, capital queue priority, discovery investment and timing of the second settlement differed. In A, omitting Ecology would make the chosen42% colony illegal; in B it is unnecessary for the two chosen colonies. The two surveyed prospects supplied an informed initial commitment without paying for repeated blind inspection flights. Scouts still had unknown branches and different reachable discovery commitments.

This is not a controlled estimate that one opening is stronger. The rewards differ (40 versus20 early science), and so do planets, geography, choices and stop cycles. Another ordinary world might justify scouting rather than early Ecology in A; the UI prefix did not establish the optimal alternative. Both plans may converge later. Two manually directed openings by the reviewer do not establish human retention, fun, general balance or repeated optimizer diversity. Neither is a complete campaign. No late-game or rival gate response was tested.

One small product wording concern was directly observed: the on-map first-mandate card still says to scout a neighboring star although A's two home neighbors are already surveyed. The new objective log/knowledge state is more specific. This is a wording consistency issue, not a failure to find a viable opening. The inspector correctly labels remote fleet presence unknown despite the free planet surveys.
