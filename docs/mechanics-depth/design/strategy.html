Make routes a political investment, then make a decisive blockade matter in negotiation

Read-only advice against main 810194596a0fa7d02efa418749bb3e27f547229e, v0.22.0, 29 October 2026. Current mechanics and both integrated good/bad design guides were inspected. Numerical examples below are proposed or explicitly labeled analytical probes; they are not a new played campaign.

Choose two connected changes: paid permanent restoration of a small number of dormant starlanes, plus state-sensitive peace reluctance when the recipient currently holds a decisive Beacon blockade. Prefer this package over industrial dispatch for the first four-hour iteration. The lane creates new geographic commitments using existing construction, exploration, movement and diplomacy; the peace adjustment gives a successful existing operation a coherent bargaining consequence.

What needs changing, and what already works

The present economy pools minerals and science empire-wide, and each treaty contributes a flat 6 credits, or 10 with Stellar Accords, regardless of geography (src/game.js:179). Geography already affects settlement races and military travel, but no infrastructure investment changes it. The actual default finish retained 1,902 credits while minerals and construction timing constrained Beacon and fleet commitments. That historical observation does not prove all current campaigns are cash-rich; it does make another passive credit bonus a weak first hypothesis.

Peace still uses age, lost colonies and observed pressure. Twelve war cycles alone satisfy Veyr/Choir, sixteen satisfy Khar (src/diplomacy.js:36–52). Current public warnings explain why a friendly civilization may contest alignment, but the peace formula never considers the contested objective. Conversely, the recorded campaigns disprove a blanket claim that the endgame lacks decisions: Odyssey suffered three alignment interruptions, and default survived real defensive battles. The target is a coherent additional choice, not an arbitrary harder ending.

1. Restore a shared dormant starlane through an ordinary colony project

Generate roughly two dormant links in a new galaxy. A player who owns one endpoint and has charted both can pay for a restoration in that endpoint colony’s ordinary production queue. On completion the link becomes a normal, permanent, bidirectional lane, usable by every faction under ordinary knowledge, border, movement, combat and order rules. It occupies no new recurring administrative system. No extra currency, civilian convoy class, automatic resource delivery or universal supply penalty is needed.

A starting cost to test is approximately 80 credits, 60 minerals and 80 work, plus the usual commissioning order. These are tuning hypotheses. At 30 production that competes with about three cycles of warship construction; at 12 production it is a much longer investment. Existing colony ownership, endpoint discovery, cost and the queue are already substantial gates. Do not add a long research prerequisite chain simply to make the feature feel larger.

Current-map analytical check: the current ASTERION generator produces 42 systems and 58 lanes. Adding hypothetical Haven↔Ashfall changes the endpoint distance from 3 hops to 1, Solace→Veyr Prime from 8 to 6, Solace→Kharos from 10 to 8, and leaves Solace→Lumen at 7. This is a pure topological calculation, with no border, fog, fleet or arrival-time claim. It demonstrates a useful scale of effect without creating a direct capital-to-capital jump.

Generation and information rules: select seeded nonduplicate links with material marginal travel savings. Check the second candidate against the first so both do not solve the same detour. Avoid guaranteed homeworld-to-homeworld attack shortcuts. Discoverable endpoint marks should invite scouting; an unknown endpoint must not silently reveal its identity or the surrounding map. On completion, explicitly communicate the new connection to observers entitled to know it. A restored road is a public geographic fact, not a source of remote fleet sensors.

Player ownership: existing plotted courses remain exactly as plotted until the player replots. A new link can improve new route previews without silently changing a saved intention. Construction pays normal costs, cancellation uses the existing unspent-work refund, capture does not grant a second restoration, and two empires cannot produce duplicate edges or double-charge a completed connection. A newly completed link cannot deliver free movement in the same construction-resolution phase.

AI participation must include an actual reason to build

The AI should use the same paid project command and queue. Estimate the gain for a small number of concrete known intentions: an existing Ark’s settlement target, a committed expedition, or reinforcement between an owned yard and a threatened colony. Require a meaningful travel reduction and a completion time before that journey matters. Respect current emergencies and Beacon funding. Do not compare omniscient paths to undiscovered enemy capitals, give the AI free restorations, or value every possible future journey as guaranteed profit. Include at least one ordinary-cost played or bounded continuation where a rival restores a useful link and then actually travels through it.

Failure modes that should stop or revise this version

  • Always restore immediately: investment has no real queue/mineral sacrifice, or shared military benefit is negligible. Tune cost or candidates before adding taxes or another counter-system.
  • Never restore: selected links save too little, completion comes after useful travel, or closed borders make all benefits hypothetical. Improve placement or shorten work rather than inflating passive rewards.
  • The road is effectively private: only its builder’s routing uses it. Then the advertised geopolitical tradeoff is absent.
  • The AI acts theatrically: it restores a road without a feasible intention, or never has enough ordinary resources to participate.
  • Late surprise with no response: completion provides hidden armies immediate extra movement, or new topology is not signalled. Preserve normal resolution timing and communicate the geographic change.

2. Let a visible, potentially decisive blockade strengthen peace reluctance

Change willingness for new proposals when all three conditions hold: the proposer publicly leads the recipient’s alignment progress; the proposer could finish within the proposed truce’s duration if alignment resumed; and the recipient currently has a military fleet blockading a participating Beacon. That last fact is already observable from the proposer’s owned world. No hidden fleet location, remote economy, route feasibility or private research needs to enter the forecast.

A bounded first formula is +24 to the existing willingness threshold, capped at 60, under those conditions. Preserve the existing lost-colony and observed-pressure scores, minimum war duration, payment and truce rules. Thus a healthy Veyr force holding the critical node at war age 12 no longer abandons it for 60 credits solely because time passed; severe losses can still justify peace, and age can still reach the capped threshold eventually. Apply the rule symmetrically to AI–AI and player–AI policy, including whether an AI originates a new peace offer.

Use “remaining steps ≤ truce duration” rather than an arbitrary 8/10 cutoff: a five-cycle truce can also shelter five successful alignment advances. Explain this as a public competitive motive, not proof of an unseen impending attack. Previously funded offers remain binding until their deadline, and signed truces remain binding. Timing acceptance of a real offer is legitimate diplomatic play.

Important disconfirmation: the prior read-only fork omitting both actual Odyssey cycle-65 peace purchases still won on 66. Its final settlements did not prove that peace removed a necessary challenge. The changed policy should be justified by the visible occupied-Beacon case, and tested against defeated, harmless and equally advanced opponents—not by a promise to make that historical ending harder.

Why industrial dispatch is the second choice for this iteration

A bounded dispatch can also create depth: an Extractor source with richness-dependent throughput buys local production at a queued Shipyard/Manufactory destination, paying minerals and distance-based credits along a committed charted path. Limit it to two links, one per source/recipient, and auto-pause without charging when the recipient is idle. Existing support buildings are enough; additional mandatory ports risk becoming a repeated gate.

It has a clean threshold reversal to test: at 8 base production a 26-work frigate needs three cycles with a nearby +4 shipment but two with a distant +5 shipment; at 9 base production both finish in two, so the cheaper nearby source becomes preferable. These fixed-current-rate examples do not prove balance. They show why throughput, distance fees and local queues could interact.

However, dispatch requires new once-per-cycle charging, construction forecasts, funding reserves, idle/capture handling, and observer-safe interruption previews. A resolver may learn that a hidden warship interrupted traffic; a read-only preview must not inspect that hidden fleet. That is doable, but it creates more integration risk than restoring an ordinary lane and reusing current movement. Do not implement both new systems in this first pass. Revisit dispatch if actual lane prototypes fail to produce different investments or targets.

A feasible four-hour scope and the evidence needed

Budget roughly 110 minutes for the dormant-link generator, queue/command/resolution/save contract and a bounded paid AI policy; 50 minutes for route-impact previews, public completion feedback, English/Russian text and compatibility; 60 minutes for actual play and contrasting-state critique; 20 minutes for targeted tuning and regression repair. The four-hour requirement is time for iteration and observation, not a reason to fill every subsystem with features.

  • Rules boundaries: deterministic generation, actual payment and work, cancellation, capture and simultaneous completion, legacy imports, no duplicate edges, ordinary travel/orders/access, and no hidden-intelligence dependence in previews.
  • Observed choices: document one case where restoration changes a settlement/expedition plan and one where declining it protects a meaningful investment. Record the sacrificed queue and the rival’s shared benefit. If both do not occur, do not call the feature proven depth.
  • Peace boundaries: decisive occupied node versus healthy ordinary peace, defeated recipient, leading recipient, exhausted war and previously funded offer. Include an actual blockade/relief continuation, not only direct state fixtures.
  • UI and resuming: a player can identify endpoints, price, duration, whose travel improves, and whether borders permit the intended journey; reopening the save preserves the project and plotted course. Check English/Russian at desktop and phone widths.
  • Engagement claim: unit tests, a generated graph and a winning policy establish correctness and feasibility. Count changed intentions and ask whether the new commitment was worth making; preserve a counterexample in the report.

Design basis: good guide §§2–4,6–10; bad guide §§1–3,6–10. In particular: situational opportunity costs, useful geography, coherent rival plans, binding commitments, readable uncertainty, and no equation between added systems and enjoyment. This advice changes no game source, guide or prior frozen evidence.
