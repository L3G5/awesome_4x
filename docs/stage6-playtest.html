Recommendation: defer generic economic programs. The useful midgame decisions were mineral timing, production sequencing, replacing infrastructure and guarding three sites. The final surplus accompanied a secure lead. A new resource sink would not, by itself, create a reason to alter that winning plan.

What the run established

FindingEvidence and implication
Minerals mattered before alignment.At cycle 52, the final beacon was 8 minerals short despite 3,605 credits and 15 orders. Waiting one cycle was necessary. A credit-to-mineral program would directly weaken this real scheduling constraint.
Production choices had real opportunity costs.Slow Sundrift/Elysian beacons began before fast Forge Solace. Exchange Ports were replaced to preserve mineral and research infrastructure. A later Sundrift mine became a shipyard for local defense. Forge II and late Academy promotions were rejected because their returns arrived after the likely finish.
Credits and orders were abundant.Credits rose from 2,106 to 5,493 despite beacons, alignment upkeep and reinforcement ships. Most of 15–17 orders went unused; the repeated warning was disabled. This demonstrates abundance in this run, not a general balance estimate.
Research actually ran out.Worldshaping completed at 62, leaving no selectable research for the final five cycles. The end state held 665 unused science at +116/cycle. A narrow post-tree choice could have a purpose in longer campaigns, but here its payoff horizon was small.
The leading network was never contested.Concord's alignment advanced every cycle from 58 to 67. All three rivals remained trade partners; no hostile fleet or diplomatic rupture involved Concord. Public wars continued elsewhere. Defensive ships were sensible insurance but were never tested.
An offensive was considered and declined.The scout observed Ophion without defending fleets. The five-frigate Elysian force had a four-jump route with two moves per cycle. Once Choir began alignment at 62, it already trailed by four cycles; leaving an own beacon exposed to blockade a trailing rival was strategically inferior.

Where to investigate next

The most useful follow-up is a small comparison of genuinely competitive alignment races, including a friendly leading empire and an existing foreign war. Determine whether rivals can make a viable intervention before changing diplomacy incentives. This single run does not justify forcing treaty betrayal: geography, defensive strength, trade and existing commitments may explain the peaceful finish. The last three cycles had no consequential decision, but arbitrary spending would add clicks rather than uncertainty.

The inability to split the existing five-frigate force also mattered: separate beacon garrisons required new hulls. Keeping the final reinforcement separate preserved flexibility. Fleet organization could improve strategic control more directly than broad economic spending programs.

Method and limits

Played 28 minutes, 19:35–20:03 UTC on 28 October 2026, at a 1440 × 960 viewport. The initial checkpoint was loaded unchanged; all decisions thereafter used Playwright clicks and key presses against the actual UI. DOM text and screenshots supplied decision information. The final campaign was exported through the game menu. Whole-state validation happened only after victory. The browser reported zero runtime errors. This is one human-style run from an already established midgame, not a human win-rate measurement or a full opening-to-finish balance study.

Postgame verification: the save validates and records Concord Transcendence at 67. The public Chronicle also records Choir alignment interruption and a new Khar–Veyr declaration at cycle 66; the ending modal superseded that final briefing. Neither involved an attack on Concord. These postgame observations were not used to make earlier decisions.
