Contract review

  • The optional merge accepts only another local, owned military formation of the same class, with enough remaining moves and a combined size at most 12. Deterministic strength/ID ordering chooses at most one partner per departure boundary.
  • The detached preflight removes that partner, combines own ships/hull, and subtracts one own order before calling the existing weighted-route candidate. It rejects a missing or slower candidate and reserves every current jump needed on the new path.
  • The paid action is the ordinary merge command. The selected fleet ID survives, the target remains fixed, and actual movement/metadata effects come from existing rules. The full observed-home-threat query runs again at each proposed absorption, including already covered threats.
  • Only current own data, public race signals and existing sensor-filtered intelligence inform the new choice. The existing stop after actual combat remains in place. Nonhostile first contact alone did not stop these expeditions before the patch and still does not; a fixed-engine baseline comparison confirms this distinction.

The weighted-route extension is particularly useful: a stronger partner would change the two-hop, one-cycle route into a three-hop, two-cycle route. The policy rejects that partner without payment, then accepts a damaged weaker partner that retains the one-cycle route. Its full result equals issuing only the successful ordinary merge and movements. Another probe puts throwing getters on unobserved enemy hull/class/count and foreign weapons/research/economy data; the decision succeeds on a frozen state without accessing them.

The full-run declaration boundary is also preserved: with three initial orders, war plus two jumps leaves no merge budget; with four orders, war plus one merge plus two jumps is legal. Both retain the selected expedition ID and target. Exact-cap merging preserves 374.06 hull rather than rounding it, and Hold, route, lead and heritage follow the ordinary command semantics.

Historical attribution and limits

The two WATCH checkpoints are replay-derived decision boundaries, not original downloaded intermediate saves. The focused test checks every archived engine fingerprint, replays all 265 recorded UI commands, verifies the actual final cycle 60 state exactly, then checks the boundary hashes for cycle 53 and cycle 56. Applying the new policy at those boundaries is a local counterfactual fork; it is not a claim that the original campaign used these merges or that a later raid must succeed.

The longer weighted-route counterexample originated in campaign_review's earlier independent synthetic audit. Its original script and result are retained under prior-independent-counterexample/, with original paths and hashes recorded in provenance.json. The core suite reproduces that synthetic route behavior; the additional weaker-partner case here extends it independently.

This is a conservative local policy, not an optimal fleet-composition planner. It does not wait for future ships, reroute other fleets to manufacture a partner, relax diplomacy thresholds, promise an interruption, or search all possible multi-merge combinations. Existing generic military merging remains outside the added rule.
