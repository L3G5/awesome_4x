The campaign
The original plan was compact and peaceful: develop Solace, Sundrift, Haven and Kepler; fund three beacons; keep useful trade partners; defend the network with a few strong formations. This remained the plan until a simultaneous alignment race made a passive finish uncertain.

The race changed the strategy
The simultaneous-finish rule was available in the frozen field guide, but absent from the Empire race panel that prompted the decision. I initially intended to honor Choir trade and simply fortify. Reading that public rule changed the assessment: a compact four-world empire could plausibly lose a tied finish to the visibly larger Choir. The foreign Legacy score was not inspected. Normal notice, scouting and staging created a three-cycle window before the limited war.

What mattered in play
Consent had strategic value
Accept, decline, reaccept, and cancel all had reasons. The western treaty opened a scout corridor. Choir’s first offer was declined during a weak military opening; the later offer was accepted after development. Veyr’s backyard settlement exposed the cost of reciprocal access. Normal notice gave a predictable withdrawal deadline.
Production timing mattered
Minerals and slots constrained the opening. Factory-first timing halved Sundrift’s contemplated charter duration. Forge II crossed a useful production threshold. Beacon replacements preserved old mines until completion. Sundrift’s late lab-to-yard conversion produced reinforcements that actually reached Haven.
Hold and division were useful
Eighteen Hold commands kept deliberate garrisons and observation posts quiet. Combat cleared Hold and prompted a useful review. Two splits moved existing ships without creating free ships or movement. Command allowed a Sundrift reinforcement to reach Haven in the same cycle.
No forced Lead usage
Each threatened world had one consolidated frigate formation. Choosing a defensive lead would not improve those engagements, so none was assigned merely to exercise the feature. Foreign lead badges were observed locally. This run supports the visibility and low distraction of the control, not a claim about its combat effectiveness.
Research still produced consequential choices: Protocol enabled the network; choosing Command before cruisers enabled timely redeployment. Cruisers completed 57 but a new cruiser would arrive too late for the imminent raid, so a one-cycle frigate was preferred. Worldshaping was queued only as a free contingency using otherwise idle science, and remained 311/600 at the finish. There was no reason to buy more late laboratories or charters.
Final visible holdings were 897 credits, 387 minerals, 13 orders, 11 completed technologies, 43 population and 698 Legacy. Sixteen frigates and one scout survived. A reserve treasury was useful insurance against trade cancellation and alignment funding; the run does not justify adding arbitrary resource sinks because some resources remained.
The actual final Empire view: five worlds, 16 frigates plus a scout, 698 Legacy and completed transcendence.
The next two improvements I would prioritize
1 · Put the tied-race rule where the race is shown
When contacted civilizations share imminent alignment progress, show the existing higher-Legacy tie rule beside the public network. Link the explanation and own score. Do not reveal hidden foreign research, fleets, population or an exact remote score merely to answer this question. Acceptance: an equal 1/10 or 9/10 public race explains the rule without requiring a guide search, and unknown rival information remains unknown.
2 · Reduce repeated ship commissioning
The longest repetitive stretch was cycles 33–40: merge the new frigate, open construction, choose Ships, commission another, end cycle. A one-click “repeat last ship commission” for an idle eligible shipyard would preserve the ordinary payment, one order, queue, and completion timing while removing repeated menu navigation. Stop at a small UI convenience; this evidence does not require a new production system or automatic spending.
The failed enemy raids deserve a separate bounded AI review, not an immediate damage buff. Veyr sent two then three frigates into defenses I reinforced to four then five; Choir tried to recover its lost beacon with two. These attacks created actual losses and forced allocation. The run alone does not establish that delayed concentration would have been feasible or better before the victory deadline. Any future policy change should use the attacker’s observed defenses and real route/time constraints.

One command annotation was corrected: event 571 said moving fleet f43, but the actual selected survivor of the preceding merge was f47. The UI receipt says “Vanguard 47 arrived at Eidolon”; the correction was recorded immediately in event 572. The portable runner derives the entire transcript from raw receipts and permits only this explicit field correction.
Five driver-selector errors remain in the raw log: an incorrect new-game selector, an incorrect trade-review selector, a missing fleet-selection control, a collision-hidden map label, and the guessed item name “fortress” instead of “bastion.” None mutated the game. A separate shell-quoting failure before the cycle 3 action batch left the discovery unresolved; the following normal End Cycle did execute. This was documented at 4 and played forward without rollback. There were zero recorded page errors or crashes.
The cycle 47 trade-income concern was a player memory error, not a UI defect: Stellar Accords had raised each treaty from 6 to 10 credits. The later UI correctly showed 10 and the research text explained the extra 4. Missing future value from a late cruiser was a planning decision, not a construction bug.
