Asterion Reach: replayability and uncertainty critique
Review date: 2026-10-29
Pinned baseline: 93a62961e994994d30c05240b17c39a418020473, v0.21.2
Served build checked: http://127.0.0.1:5220 (same commit/version)
Scope: randomness, repeated openings, geography, exploration, faction identity, viable plans, and late-game repetition. No game source was edited.

MAIN FINDING
The current game has real situational strategy, especially in travel, colony specialization, treaty access, and protecting a public network. It is not supported to call its maps cosmetic, its military path nonviable, or its late game universally boring. Its clearest replayability limitation is a familiar starting geography and discovery scaffold: many campaigns begin by asking different numerical versions of a recognizable local question. Existing generation already creates some useful research/settlement reversals, but the fixed AI research order does not exploit them.

The implementation priorities below are hypotheses to test. Neither automated wins nor the three historical manually directed UI campaigns establish human enjoyment or replay longevity.

METHOD AND EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES
Read both supplied good-4x.html and bad-4x.html guides. Applied their distinction between a changed plan and cosmetic novelty, between input and post-commitment randomness, and between simulation correctness and engagement.

Inspected current src/game.js, content.js, development.js, discoveries.js, ai.js, navigation.js, victory.js, and relevant main.js UI. New checks:
- audit.mjs: 64 fixed seeds for map structure, local resources and discoveries; first 12 seeds also run with the ordinary shared AI controlling the player and ordinary rivals through completed campaigns. All sampled states validated. Full results and source fingerprints are in audit-results.json.
- ui-openings.mjs: two independent fresh browser contexts on the served baseline, using visible UI controls only. Created replayability-review-9 and replayability-review-4, moved the scout to Sundrift and initial frigate to Asterion, inspected research, and stopped during cycle 1. No imported or modified save and no hidden state reads. Six screenshots and exact inspector text are retained; zero page errors. These are bounded opening observations, not full playtests or human-subject observations.
- Existing scripts/campaign-benchmark.mjs rerun on the current baseline: 20 fixed seeds for each of passive, science, military and balanced policies, 80 total. Completed in 38.8 seconds with no invalid saves or negative resources. Full data in policy-benchmark.json; compact results in policy-summary.json. Do not pool this with the first 12 runs as independent evidence.

Read historical default-campaign, odyssey-playtest and watch-playtest reports. Those reports describe actual UI play on their labeled older builds. I did not independently replay them in this review and do not relabel their experience as current v0.21.2 play. Their reports and exact traces supply concrete counterexamples to simplistic design claims, with version limits retained. Also checked the military-playtest outcome when judging whether conquest had ever been demonstrated.

OBSERVED STRENGTHS
1. Geography affects timing and target choice. High confidence about mechanics and measured variation; medium about player impact.
The map remains connected by construction, with short extra lanes. Across 64 seeds it has 51-61 edges, 108 distinct possible observed edges, and edge-set Jaccard similarity against ASTERION of .436-.611 (mean .534). Solace-to-Veyr Prime shortest paths range from 5 to 20 jumps; Solace-to-Lumen from 5 to 19. Those are materially different travel and reinforcement commitments, not just shifted drawings. Source: game.js:123-138; audit-results.json mapSummary/maps.
The strongest historical example is Odyssey: preserving Stillwater's Observatory did not secure it. Khar settled it before the distant Ark arrived, and Concord redirected to Kepler. WATCH used trade to reopen a scout corridor, then limited conquest of a rival beacon to change a tied race. Distance, ownership and access mattered in actual choices reported by those campaigns.

2. Local properties can already reverse useful priorities. High confidence about availability; unproven preferred optimal plan.
In replayability-review-9, only one unowned world within two jumps of Solace is initially settleable: Sundrift (76%, ore 3, science 4). Haven is 44%, Asterion 38%, Eidolon 21%, Halcyon 22%. Ecology would open Haven and Asterion. In replayability-review-4, seven worlds within two jumps meet the ordinary 50% threshold, including Haven (87%, ore 5, science 3) and Peregrine (70%, ore 5, science 2). Early Ecology has a plausible immediate expansion use in the former and a much weaker local use in the latter. This is a candidate reversal, not a demonstrated optimal build order.
The current UI faithfully showed Asterion at 38% vs 62%, and the different Sundrift discovery types, before any research commitment. Research assignment is free and changing research refunds invested progress (game.js:467-472), so the player can scout before committing without a sunk-progress trap.

3. Discoveries have readable choices and some genuine persistence. High confidence about code, medium about recurring interest.
Each discovered option discloses its cost/reward. Garden can cross a settlement threshold; the recovered escort has an operational use. Observatory/Reef require an owned colony, supporting infrastructure, safe operation, and later construction or portable reward. History follows the world through transfer. This is more connected than an interchangeable notification grant. Odyssey's lost Observatory and WATCH's deliberate remote-Reef salvage are useful historical counterexamples to the claim that preservation is always correct. The tradeoff includes location and time, not only reward size.

4. Multiple constraints and endings matter. High confidence about demonstrated mechanics; low about comparative balance.
In the current 20-seed science policy, Protocol arrives at median cycle 37 versus balanced 47, but alignment starts at median 60 versus 59. Research lead alone does not pay for and build the network. The current military controller produced one Domination and one Transcendence win; historical IRON-COVENANT-10 achieved Domination at 57 without commissioning player beacons. Military viability cannot be dismissed. The benchmark's aggressive controller also permits simultaneous wars, so its 15 defeats cannot establish that the military rules are weak.

OBSERVED LIMITATIONS AND DESIGN HYPOTHESES
A. The local scaffold is much more repeatable than the overall lane metrics suggest. High confidence about the invariants; medium about boredom risk.
All four capitals keep the same names and positions. Nonhome worlds keep the same spiral order and radial positions, with bounded angular/coordinate perturbations (game.js:105-121). Every one of the 64 maps has both Solace-Sundrift and Solace-Asterion. Solace neighbors come from just four observed sets, all subsets of {Sundrift, Haven, Halcyon, Asterion}. The opening safety conversion uses the first unowned adjacency, rather than evaluating competing opportunities; it chooses Sundrift in 57/64 maps. Every map has anomalies at exactly s4,s8,s12,s16,s20,s24,s28,s32,s36,s40. The two staged sites replace two of those slots (discoveries.js:28-38).
An experienced player can therefore anticipate where to look for early rewards and which familiar branches to investigate. This is a constrained unknown map, not a fresh geographic situation each time. Shuffling only names or adding more reward types would not address that limitation.

B. A rigid controller can conceal the adaptation already present. High confidence about policy; not evidence of human optimality.
The first 12 ordinary AI-player probes all settled Sundrift first and completed Logistics first. Eleven completed the identical full research sequence; the twelfth stopped one technology short. That is expected from ai.js:892-897, which uses a fixed faction priority list except for alignment-income recovery. The observation does NOT show that a human should always do this. It shows that this automated evidence is weak for evaluating flexible openings and that rivals' research personality is largely independent of surveyed geography.

C. Much discovery variation still consists of early portable resources. High confidence about authored rules; unknown long-run fatigue.
Eight of ten map slots use one-stage events. Several reward choices grant currencies that feed the same expansion/research engine. Grants are meaningful: 85 minerals exceeds an Ark's 55-mineral construction cost; 75 science exceeds any foundational research cost. This is input randomness with explicit response choices, not an unknowable lottery at the click. However, repeated nearby copies can become similar priorities: replayability-review-4 has four Wrecks within two jumps, at Sundrift, Haven, Eidolon and Peregrine. No evidence here establishes that such a cluster decides a race or that a shuffled deck would improve the game. Do not infer balance or engagement from the grant sizes alone.

D. Faction identity primarily changes opponents, not the replayed player role. High confidence about scope.
The player always controls Concord; the new-campaign UI accepts only a seed. Opponents have credit/science/damage bonuses and different research priorities, with the same core techs, ships and buildings. Their identities produce different timing pressures. They do not currently offer alternate player rule systems. This is a scope limitation, not a demand to implement faction selection. More percentage bonuses would not automatically create different viable plans.

E. The late game narrows, but boring cleanup is not established. High confidence about scope; low about human fatigue.
All 20 current science-policy players completed all technologies by campaign finish; 17/20 balanced did. Development value changes late, and the network becomes the concrete goal. Yet the actual historical evidence repeatedly renews decisions: default's final Haven battle, WATCH's tie-breaking limited war, Odyssey's three interruptions and relief campaigns. Those are not uncontested clicks to a fixed final cycle. Odyssey nevertheless reports substantial repeated commissioning, travel and merging; WATCH reports a repetitive frigate-production stretch. Those are concrete administration concerns, but some affected controls have since improved. I did not observe a current full late campaign, so I cannot give a current fatigue verdict or recommend another crisis merely because technology runs out.

PRIORITIZED FEASIBLE IMPROVEMENTS (PROTOTYPE, THEN KEEP ONLY IF THE TASK IMPROVES)
1. Give local geography a less predictable scaffold while preserving its safety guarantee.
Player problem: on repeat play, the first search is constrained to the same small named neighborhood and known anomaly slots. Proposed bounded experiment: modify only generation, using a seeded angular phase and/or bounded radial reassignment of nonhome systems relative to the four capitals, then keep the existing connected-lane construction and evaluate the safety world after the neighborhood is formed. Retain 42 worlds, four empires, ordinary yields/rewards, and at least one accessible 50%+ unowned first colony. Preserve imported existing saves without regeneration. This is one map-generation experiment, not a faction/content expansion.
Expected effect: players sometimes prefer a compact ore route, sometimes an outward science/settlement opportunity, and sometimes an access/defense corridor. The important difference is the price of following the old plan, not the selected system name.
Test: preserve ASTERION as a documented old-save compatibility case. Review the original 64 seeds plus two manually directed repeated openings with the same victory goal. Record first colony, second colony, research/build commitment, reinforcement path, reason for the first divergence, and what is sacrificed by insisting on the previous plan. Check connectedness, minimum useful opening, worst local access, early jackpot proximity, and rival travel. Acceptance requires at least two credible plan reversals explained by timing/access/role. Merely changing Sundrift into another label, or making the new guaranteed world always the compulsory first move, is a failed result. Counterexample: current seeds review-9 and review-4 already prompt distinct Ecology choices; if experienced players find that sufficient and the new generator only adds reconnaissance time or unfair isolation, prefer the current map and reject this prototype.
Confidence: high that current scaffold is invariant, medium-low that this change is the best fun improvement. Avoid a broad map rewrite without the bounded comparison.

2. Let early rival research respond to revealed expansion constraints within faction priorities.
Player problem: local map variation has less effect on opponent plans than it could; the fixed priority controller cannot assess the very reversal the map generated. Proposed small AI change: when idle research is chosen, allow surveyed, reachable, unowned 30-49% settlement prospects and a shortage of 50%+ prospects to raise Ecology priority; delay that adjustment when several ordinary viable settlement opportunities remain. Use charted/remembered or locally observed information only and preserve faction personality and all ordinary prices. This consumes existing strategic differences rather than adding bonuses or random tech offers.
Test: controlled paired scenarios derived from the review-9/review-4 distinction, with the AI's actual knowledge and travel budgets recorded. Ecology should change only when it enables an achievable settlement plan. Trace research -> Ark production -> reachable settlement, rather than checking the tech selection alone. Include a short war threat where Weapons remains preferable and a distant or hostile harsh world that must not trigger the branch. Reject the change if it merely front-loads Ecology without reaching useful colonies, if it becomes the new unconditional script, or if it delays necessary defense. Then compare a small fixed-seed cohort and one visible campaign for actual changed interaction with the player.
Confidence: high about current rigidity, medium about this branch improving opponent coherence, low about whether players notice more replay variety. This is an alternative first iteration to the map experiment; do not combine both before measuring cause.

QUESTIONS THAT REMAIN OPEN
- Does an experienced optimizer actually change the opening between review-9 and review-4? What is the first action whose preferred answer reverses, and what would make it reverse back?
- When repeating one faction and one victory goal, how soon does a player recognize the map/reward scaffold, and does that recognition reduce interest or support enjoyable mastery?
- Are science/military/economic plans comparably viable with competent, goal-directed control? The benchmark controllers are neither equally skilled nor optimized for those goals.
- Do extreme early grants determine outcomes after alternatives, recovery and travel are considered? No causal counterfactual was run.
- When does a player consider a current campaign decided, relative to formal victory? Do they enjoy the last defense, or regard it as compulsory production work?
- Do roleplayers value the existing repeated discovery texts and persistent world histories differently from optimizers? No human preference evidence was collected.
