The actual ending. Khar’s public alignment was 9/10, Choir’s 6/10 and Veyr’s 4/10 when the Concord completed 10/10. This was not a tied finish.
Worlds with different jobs
Sundrift became the mineral foundation. Nacre’s low habitability was acceptable because its native science justified an Academy. Solace’s Forge pushed production from 22 to 29, crossing the threshold for a one-cycle frigate. Meridian supplied ore before receiving its research infrastructure; Kepler became a practical replacement for a distant settlement plan.
The comparison panels mattered when slots filled. Two Habitation Domes were eventually replaced: the previews showed that existing citizens remained at 12/8, with growth paused. Once all twelve technologies were complete, wartime shipyards replaced laboratories at Sundrift and Nacre. Nacre’s preview explicitly disclosed the loss of its Academy charter.
A production threshold, not simply a bigger number: the Forge enabled one-cycle frigates.
The observatory we did not inherit
The preserved record belongs to the world. It remembers both civilizations’ decisions.
At cycle 20, the scout paid 40 credits and 20 minerals to preserve the Last Observatory on fertile Stillwater. A Colony Ark was later sent along an eight-jump route. Khar settled the world at cycle 27 while the Ark was still six jumps away.
The expedition changed destination and settled Kepler instead. The lost opportunity did not justify an unprepared war. Later local observation showed Khar had completed the observatory’s story at cycle 35, taking the atlas reward. The ending linked back to that exact shared history.
This setback made distance, settlement timing and ownership consequential. No restoration or reward was invented for the player.
The race became a war
Early diplomacy was explicit: the Concord initiated Veyr and Khar trade, and accepted a funded Choir offer at cycle 8. All three Concord beacons were operational at cycle 51. Veyr and Choir broke their trade treaties and declared war during 53→54. Their subsequent attacks forced real fleet assembly, repairs and industrial changes.

By cycle 65, prolonged-war peace settlements were available. Paying 60 credits and one order to each enemy secured binding truces and withdrew the visible Veyr force from Kepler. The final alignment payment then completed the campaign at cycle 66. Khar remained a trade partner throughout.
What the interface helped—and where it still asked for work
Meaningful support
Investment comparisons exposed production thresholds, capacity tradeoffs and lost charter support. Quick commissions shortened repeated rebuilding. Merge previews prevented movement assumptions during relief. Hold kept established garrisons out of movement cycling. Battle rounds explained the actual exchanges without implying that nearby formations fought together.
The ending was reopened from both menu and Chronicle. Its battle, expedition and world links worked. Three final exports, taken before and after inspection, are byte-identical.
Remaining friction
The late war still required navigating among shipyards, moving new formations and merging them manually. This was useful control during a close race, but a substantial portion of the late interaction count. The unused-order warning also remained a repeated confirmation when deliberate reserves and stationary garrisons were appropriate.
One real player mistake was preserved: unfinished Shields research at 212/220 was switched to Accords, then corrected the next cycle. Seven driver failures are separately recorded; none is presented as a game crash or erased by a rollback.
Evidence, without reconstructed history masquerading as play
The original evidence is frozen. Every game mutation occurred through visible controls, including end-cycle confirmation. Observer-safe projections supplied current own assets, charted geography and locally visible fleets. No save edits, resource grants, engine commands, policy automation, rollback or browser recovery were used during play.
